01 · The problem

A 30-year paper process, carried on someone's back.

Compartamos doesn't work like a traditional bank. Its core product, the crédito grupal, requires forming groups of 5 to 50 people who know each other personally, all borrowing at the same time, all paying together. If one member falls behind, the group covers. It's the mechanism that keeps the bank's default rate below 3% in a market where the average exceeds 10%.

The problem was operational. Every day, thousands of field agents - called promotores - walked their assigned zones carrying backpacks weighing over 10kg of paper forms, ID photocopies, business photographs, and handwritten ledgers. Finding a potential group, validating each member, collecting documents, and returning to the branch to digitize everything took between 5 and 8 business days for a small group. Larger groups could wait up to 20.

The constraint that made this hard

Every step had a compliance checkpoint: KYC verification, INE/IFE validation, CURP lookup, business evidence, risk scoring and legal sign-off. Any digital solution had to keep every one of them while removing the physical back-and-forth. CNBV doesn't negotiate.

02 · Research

40% of the trips to the branch were habit, not regulation.

[My call] Before touching a wireframe, I ran ethnographic field research across multiple regions: riding along with promotores, sitting through branch digitization sessions and interviewing Directores, Subdirectores and Ejecutivos de Crédito with 10 to 20 years in the process. The question wasn't "how do we digitize this?" It was "which parts are compliance-required and which are just habit?"

Roughly 40% of the trips back to the branch existed because of habit and legacy system limits, not because CNBV required them. That 40% is where the cycle compression was hiding.

Ethnographic research

Field shadowing

Rode along on daily routes in urban and semi-rural zones and mapped where agents went, what they carried, where they waited and where the process broke.

Stakeholder alignment

War Room

[My call] Risk, Legal, Finance and Technology in one room, one rule: every "no" came with a counter-proposal. The Service Blueprint was the shared source of truth.

Compliance mapping

Regulatory decomposition

Tagged every step with the AML/CFT team as CNBV-required, internally required or habit. The third group was our design freedom. [Team] Legal sign-off.

What the research actually found

The promotores weren't frustrated by the paperwork itself. They were frustrated by uncertainty: they didn't know if a prospect would pass risk scoring until after two days of collecting documents. The insight that unlocked the design: move the risk pre-check to step one. If the system could flag a likely approval before the visit, they would carry nothing until approval was confirmed.

03 · Design system & product

Three decisions, each traced back to the field.

[My call] There was no Design System when I arrived. I built one from scratch for three channels (field app, Contact Center and Administrator Portal) under one constraint: low-end Android devices, variable light, and users who weren't always looking at the screen. Three decisions shaped the product.

1 · Certainty before paperwork

Research named uncertainty, not paper, as the real cost, so the flow was reordered around the risk pre-check: the system flags a likely approval before the promotor visits, and documents come after. [My call] The app was built offline-first (sync on connection, full functionality without it) because rural zones frequently had no 4G signal.

2 · The script is the interface

Contact Center agents run the same KYC and data-capture flow by phone. Speech prompts embedded in the UI tell them what to say at each step, which cut errors and training time at once. The same script delivers the legally required oral privacy notice (LFPDPPP).

3 · One app, every role

A promotor needs their own clients, a Subgerente their team, a Gerente the region. [My call] Instead of separate apps, RBAC decides what each role sees and does: one codebase, role-appropriate depth.

Contact Center - Prospect Manager

Beyond the data-capture and call-wrap screens shown above, the Contact Center tool included a dedicated Gestor de Prospectos - a prospect search and tracking module that allowed agents to look up existing prospects by name, folio, or date of birth before initiating a new call. This prevented duplicate records, enabled follow-up on previously contacted prospects, and surfaced the current pipeline status directly in the agent's view.

[My call] The data model behind the interface

The prospect table was designed around a status taxonomy I defined with the operations team: Perfilamiento (initial qualification), Validación (identity verification), Llamada cortada (call dropped - requires follow-up), and Creación de prospecto (successfully registered). Each status drove a different next action for the agent, color-coded inline so a 2-second scan was enough to know what to do next without reading the detail.

04 · Regulatory compliance

Every checkpoint, preserved. Every trip to the branch, eliminated.

The regulatory work ran in parallel with design, not after it.

KYC & AML/CFT · [My call]
End-to-end digital identity verification: INE/IFE capture by camera, CURP lookup and biometric confirmation, signed off by the AML/CFT team before launch. Legally equivalent to the paper process step for step, 80% faster in practice.
LFPDPPP consent
Consent architecture and privacy notice placement in the app and the Contact Center, defined with the DPO. In the Contact Center, the speech script delivers the oral notice.
RBAC & access control · [My call]
Permission hierarchies for the Administrator Portal (promotor, subgerente, director) reviewed and approved by Internal Audit and Information Security for 15,000+ users.

05 · Results

The numbers.

8d → 2h Credit origination cycle 90% reduction in cycle time. Later optimized to 40 minutes in the fully digital flow.
15,000+ Field agents on the system Promotores and Asesores across urban and rural zones, including low-connectivity areas.
74.3% Field satisfaction after launch Overall score across 113 asesores in the Occidente region, measured with SUS, CSAT and NPS by the VoC program.
<3% Default rate maintained The digital process preserved the group credit model's risk structure. CNBV sign-off obtained without compliance incidents.
What happened after launch

The research kept going. In every region, the lowest-scoring flow was the handoff from field to Contact Center, not either channel on its own. That finding set the Q1 2024 roadmap.

06 · Closing

The hardest part wasn't the product.

It was convincing leadership that research takes time and that rework costs more than discovery. Results had been committed at board level before the team existed. I spent my first month negotiating a realistic timeline with the Service Blueprint as evidence, and won that argument before touching a single wireframe.
